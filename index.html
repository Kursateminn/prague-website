Welcome to Prague

Prague is the capital city of Czechia and one of the most historic cities in Europe. It is located in Central Europe, on both sides of the Vltava River. Prague is also known as the “Golden City” and the “City of a Hundred Spires.” The history of Prague goes back to the Middle Ages. Especially Prague Castle, Charles Bridge, Old Town Square, and the Astronomical Clock are among the most important symbols of the city. Prague is a special city where Gothic, Baroque, and Renaissance architecture can be seen together. Food culture is also an important part of Prague. Meat dishes, soups, and pastries are very common. One of the most famous meals is goulash. Also, trdelník, a sweet pastry sold on the streets, is very popular among tourists. Czechia is also famous for its beer culture, and Prague has many historic beer halls and local restaurants. When visiting Prague, people should explore not only historical places but also narrow streets, riverside views, cafés, and squares. With its historical atmosphere, Prague makes visitors feel the spirit of old Europe.

1) Charles Bridge

History of Charles Bridge Charles Bridge is one of the most famous landmarks in Prague and one of the oldest stone bridges in Europe. The bridge was built in 1357 during the reign of King Charles IV, who wanted to connect Prague Castle with the Old Town. The bridge crosses the Vltava River and is about 516 meters long. For many centuries, it was the most important connection between the two sides of Prague. Architecture and Statues Charles Bridge is famous for its beautiful Gothic architecture and its 30 baroque statues placed along the sides of the bridge. One of the most popular statues is the statue of St. John of Nepomuk. According to legend, touching the statue brings good luck and means you will return to Prague one day. The bridge towers at both ends are also important historical structures and offer amazing views of the city. Atmosphere Today, Charles Bridge is one of the most visited places in Prague. Visitors can see street musicians, artists, painters, and souvenir sellers on the bridge. Early in the morning and during sunset, the bridge becomes especially beautiful and offers fantastic views of Prague Castle and the Vltava River. Walking across Charles Bridge allows people to feel the historical and romantic atmosphere of Prague.

  • Construction started in 1357.

  • The bridge survived many floods and wars.

  • It was the only bridge over the Vltava River for hundreds of years.

  • Charles Bridge is considered one of the symbols of Prague.

  • The best time to visit is early morning or sunset for fewer crowds and better views.

2 ) Prague Castle

Prague Castle is one of the largest ancient castles in the world and the most important historical symbol of Prague. It was founded in the 9th century and became the center of Czech kings, emperors, and presidents. The castle complex includes palaces, churches, gardens, and the famous St. Vitus Cathedral. It is located on a hill above the Vltava River and offers one of the best views in Prague. Prague Castle is known for its Gothic architecture, rich history, and beautiful atmosphere. Today, it is one of the most visited tourist attractions in Czechia.

Important Facts:

  • Founded in the 9th century

  • One of the largest castle complexes in the world

  • Home of Czech presidents today

  • Includes St. Vitus Cathedral

  • UNESCO World Heritage Site

  • Famous for panoramic views of Prague


































































































3 ) Old Town Square



Old Town Square is the historical heart of Prague and one of the most beautiful squares in Europe. It has been the center of city life since the 12th century. The square is surrounded by colorful buildings, churches, cafés, and historical monuments. One of the most famous attractions here is the Astronomical Clock, which was installed in 1410 and is one of the oldest working astronomical clocks in the world. Visitors can also see the Church of Our Lady before Týn and the statue of religious reformer Jan Hus in the square. Old Town Square is especially popular during Christmas and summer because of its festivals, street performances, and markets.

Important Facts

  1. Historical center of Prague
  2. Founded in the 12th century
  3. Home to the Astronomical Clock
  4. Famous for Gothic and Baroque architecture
  5. Popular for festivals and Christmas markets
  6. One of the most visited places in Prague







4 ) St. Vitus Cathedral

St. Vitus Cathedral is the most important church in Prague and one of the greatest examples of Gothic architecture in Europe. It is located inside Prague Castle and has been the spiritual symbol of Czechia for centuries. The cathedral is famous for its tall towers, beautiful stained-glass windows, and impressive interior. Many Czech kings and emperors were crowned here. Construction of the cathedral started in 1344 and continued for hundreds of years. Today, it is one of the most visited places in Prague and offers amazing views from its tower.








5 ) Astronomical Clock


The Prague Astronomical Clock, also known as the “Orloj,” is one of the oldest working astronomical clocks in the world. It was installed in 1410 on the wall of the Old Town Hall in Old Town Square. The clock shows the time, date, positions of the sun and moon, and different astronomical details. Every hour, visitors gather to watch the famous moving figures and the “Walk of the Apostles” show. The Astronomical Clock is one of the most important symbols of Prague and attracts millions of tourists


About Prague Astronomical Clock:


  1. Installed in 1410
  2. One of the oldest working astronomical clocks
  3. Located in Old Town Square
  4. Famous for its hourly moving statue show
  5. Symbol of Prague’s history and science

6 )National Theatre

The National Theatre in Prague is one of the most important cultural buildings in Czechia. It was opened in 1881 and became a symbol of Czech national identity and art. The theatre is famous for its beautiful Neo-Renaissance architecture, golden roof, and impressive interior decorations. It is located near the Vltava River and is one of Prague’s most iconic landmarks. Today, the National Theatre hosts opera, ballet, and drama performances and continues to play an important role in Czech culture.

  1. Opened in 1881

  2. Symbol of Czech culture and nationalism

  3. Famous for Neo-Renaissance architecture

  4. Located near the Vltava River

  5. Hosts opera, ballet, and theatre performances

  6. One of Prague’s cultural landmarks

7 ) St. Nicholas Church (Mala Strana)

x

St. Nicholas Church is one of the most beautiful Baroque churches in Prague. It is located in the Malá Strana (Lesser Town) district, near Prague Castle. The church was built in the 18th century and is famous for its large green dome, impressive interior, and beautiful fresco paintings. Inside the church, visitors can see grand chandeliers, detailed decorations, and one of the largest organs in Europe. According to history, the famous composer Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart once played the organ here after visiting Prague. Today, St. Nicholas Church is an important historical and cultural landmark and also hosts classical music concerts.

8 ) Dancing House

The Dancing House is one of the most modern and unique buildings in Prague. It was designed by architects Frank Gehry and Vlado Milunić and completed in 1996. The building is famous for its unusual shape, which looks like two dancers moving together. Because of this design, it is also called “Fred and Ginger,” inspired by the famous dancers Fred Astaire and Ginger Rogers. Located near the Vltava River, the Dancing House represents modern architecture in a city mostly known for its historical buildings. Today, it includes offices, a restaurant, and a rooftop terrace with beautiful views of Prague.

9 ) Church of Saint Nicholas (in Old Town)

The Church of Saint Nicholas is a beautiful Baroque church located in Old Town Square in Prague. It was built in the 18th century and is known for its elegant white exterior and impressive interior decorations. The church is famous for its large chandelier, artistic ceiling paintings, and historical organ music concerts. Today, it is one of the important cultural and historical landmarks in Prague and attracts many visitors every year.

10 ) Tyn Church

The Church of Our Lady before Týn, also known as Týn Church, is one of the most famous Gothic landmarks in Prague. Construction of the church began in the 14th century and continued for many years, combining Gothic architecture with later Baroque elements. Its two unequal towers, rising above Old Town Square, symbolize the masculine and feminine sides of the world according to local legends. The church was designed by medieval architects inspired by French Gothic cathedrals, featuring pointed arches, detailed stonework, and impressive interior decorations. Today, Týn Church remains an important symbol of Prague’s historical and architectural heritage.

11 ) Powder Tower

The Powder Tower is one of the most important Gothic monuments in Prague and served as one of the original city gates in the 15th century. Built in 1475, the tower was designed by architect Matěj Rejsek and later completed with Neo-Gothic details. It was originally intended to be a ceremonial entrance for Czech kings traveling to Prague Castle during coronation processions. In the 17th century, the tower was used to store gunpowder, which is how it received the name “Powder Tower.” Today, visitors admire its dark Gothic architecture, detailed stone carvings, and panoramic views of Prague from the top.

12 ) Prague 2

Prague 2 is one of the most charming and historic districts of Prague, blending elegant architecture, peaceful green spaces, and deep cultural heritage. The area is famous for its beautiful streets, traditional cafés, and historic atmosphere that reflects the soul of the Czech capital. One of its greatest treasures is the ancient Vyšehrad fortress, dramatically located on a hill overlooking the Vltava River. According to Czech legends, Vyšehrad was the birthplace of Prague and the first residence of Czech rulers. Surrounded by massive stone walls, quiet gardens, and scenic walking paths, the fortress offers breathtaking panoramic views of the city skyline. Visitors can explore the stunning Basilica of St. Peter and St. Paul, discover the resting place of many famous Czech artists and writers in the historic cemetery, and enjoy the peaceful atmosphere that makes Vyšehrad one of Prague’s most magical and unforgettable locations.

13 ) Kafka's Head

The Kafka's Head is one of the most fascinating modern artworks in Prague, combining technology, art, and literature in a unique way. Created by the famous Czech artist David Černý, the massive moving sculpture was installed in 2014 as a tribute to the legendary writer Franz Kafka, who was born in Prague. Made from 42 rotating stainless-steel layers, the sculpture continuously transforms and reshapes Kafka’s face, symbolizing the complexity, uncertainty, and fragmented identity often found in Kafka’s literary works. Located near the modern shopping and business district of Prague, Kafka’s Head creates a striking contrast between historical Prague and contemporary artistic expression, making it one of the city’s most memorable and symbolic landmarks.

14 ) Food

Cafe Louvre

Café Louvre is one of those places in Prague that truly makes you feel the spirit of the city. Located on Národní Street, just a short walk from the Old Town and the Vltava River, this historic café has been welcoming visitors since 1902. The moment you walk inside, the elegant interior, high ceilings, and classic atmosphere make it feel like stepping back into old Prague. What makes Café Louvre special is not only its beautiful design, but also its history — famous figures such as Franz Kafka and Albert Einstein once spent time here. Sitting by the window with a coffee and a traditional Czech dessert while watching the streets of Prague creates a calm and unforgettable feeling. It is the perfect place to relax after exploring the city and experience Prague in a more authentic and timeless way.

Cafe Slavia

Café Slavia is one of the most beautiful and peaceful cafés in Prague. It is located near the Vltava River and has an amazing view of the National Theatre and Prague Castle. When you sit by the window with a coffee or a dessert, the atmosphere feels calm and artistic. For many years, writers, poets, and artists spent time here. One of the most famous visitors was Nazım Hikmet. During his time in Prague, he came to Café Slavia often to write, think, and enjoy the city. Knowing that such an important poet sat in the same place makes the café feel even more special. Today, Café Slavia is still loved by both locals and tourists. Some people come for the famous hot chocolate, some for the traditional Czech desserts, and others simply to enjoy the warm and nostalgic feeling of the café. It is not just a place to drink coffee — it is a place where history, art, and peaceful moments come together.

Tredelnik

When I visited Prague, one of the most famous street foods I tried was Trdelník. The sweet smell of cinnamon and sugar was everywhere in the old streets of the city. Freshly baked over hot coals, it quickly became one of my favorite snacks in Prague. Trdelník has an interesting history. Many people think it is originally from Prague, but its roots actually come from Central Europe, especially from areas connected to Hungary and Slovakia. The dessert became popular centuries ago and was made during festivals and special celebrations. The dough is wrapped around a wooden stick, slowly cooked over fire, and then covered with sugar and walnuts. Today, Prague made Trdelník famous all around the world. In the city center, especially near Old Town Square and the small historic streets, you can see many bakeries making it fresh in front of visitors. Some modern versions are filled with ice cream, chocolate, or cream, but the classic cinnamon and sugar taste still feels the most traditional and authentic.
